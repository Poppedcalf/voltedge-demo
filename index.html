The starting point

Most energy decisions start with a problem, not a product.

Before choosing panels, inverters or batteries, most customers are trying to answer a simpler question: what do we need our power to do?

VoltEdge Energy is designed around that starting point. Understand the need first, then explore the options.

  1. 01

    Power that can’t be relied on

    Interruptions to supply affect work, study and household routines. Many customers begin by asking how to keep the essentials running.

  2. 02

    A need for backup power

    Some properties need continuity during outages more than they need to generate large amounts of electricity. The answer depends on what has to stay on.

  3. 03

    Energy requirements for a home or business

    A household and a workshop use power very differently. Understanding daily use and future plans comes before choosing equipment.

FAQs

Questions customers often ask.

Short, plain answers. Every property is different, so a proper assessment always comes before a recommendation.

How is a suitable solar system determined?

It starts with how your property uses electricity: which appliances or equipment matter most, when you use them and what you want to achieve, such as reducing reliance on the grid or keeping essentials running during outages. Roof or ground space, shading and budget are considered alongside this. A proper assessment is needed before any system can be recommended.

What is the difference between solar panels and backup batteries?

Solar panels generate electricity when the sun is shining. Batteries store electrical energy so it can be used later, for example during an outage or in the evening. Some systems use panels alone, some use batteries with an inverter, and some combine them. Which combination suits you depends on your needs.

What information is needed for a quotation?

Typically: whether the property is residential or commercial, what you want to power or protect, a rough idea of how you use electricity (an electricity bill helps if you have one), the type and condition of the roof or available space, and any budget or timing considerations. If you are unsure about some of these, say so. You can still start the conversation.

Are residential and commercial requirements different?

Often, yes. Commercial properties can have different operating hours, larger or more varied loads, and different consequences when power is interrupted. Residential projects usually focus on household routines and comfort. That is why the enquiry form asks which one applies.

How is installation planned, and what about maintenance?

Once a quotation is accepted, installation is planned around the property, access and equipment availability. Maintenance needs vary from system to system, so it is worth discussing aftercare and servicing arrangements before you decide, not after.
